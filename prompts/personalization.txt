Write a short outreach email from a university student asking about an
internship or industrial attachment.

# THE STUDENT

{student_profile}

This person is a STUDENT with no professional work experience. Write as
someone early in their learning, who is capable and willing to work, and
honest about their level.

# THE ORGANISATION

Name: {company_name}
What they do: {what_they_do}
Their technology function: {technology_function}
Evidence about internships: {internship_evidence}

This campaign should emphasise: {campaign_focus}

# WHAT YOU MUST NOT WRITE

Do not invent anything. Every factual statement about this organisation
must come from the three fields above. If a field says UNKNOWN, you know
nothing about it -- write around it, do not fill the gap.

Specifically, never write:

- Flattery or compliments. No "I have long admired your company", no
  "your innovative work", no "industry-leading", no "I am impressed by".
  If you cannot say something factual, say nothing.
- Invented specifics: projects, products, achievements, funding, awards,
  clients, technologies, team sizes, or office locations.
- A claim that they have an internship programme, unless the evidence
  field above says so.
- A named person. You do not know who will read this.
- Claims about the student beyond the profile above. No invented
  projects, no invented grades, no fabricated experience.
- The student's year of study. The profile gives a graduation year, NOT
  a current year. Never write "second-year", "third-year", "final-year"
  or similar -- you do not know, and guessing misrepresents them to an
  employer. Say what the profile says: the degree, the university, and
  the expected graduation year.

# HOW TO DESCRIBE THE STUDENT'S LEVEL

Be accurate, not modest and not inflated.

Correct: "I am studying data science and have been working with Python
and SQL through my coursework and personal projects."

Wrong, too senior: "I am an experienced data scientist with a strong
track record of delivering machine learning solutions."

Wrong, too weak: "I do not have much to offer but would be grateful for
any chance."

The student is a capable learner, not an expert. Say what they have
actually worked with. Never describe them as experienced, seasoned,
proven, or accomplished.

# THE EMAIL

Structure, in about 120-160 words total:

1. One sentence: who they are and what they study.
2. One or two sentences: why this organisation specifically -- grounded
   ONLY in the factual fields above. If you genuinely have nothing
   specific, say plainly that you are interested in the kind of work
   they do, and name that work.
3. One or two sentences: what the student can contribute and what they
   want to learn. Be concrete about skills.
4. A clear, modest ask: whether they would consider an intern or
   industrial attachment placement, and who to speak to if not them.
5. A short sign-off.

IMPORTANT about the sign-off: end with a closing line only, such as
"Thank you for your time." or "Kind regards,". Do NOT write the
student's name after it. A signature block with their name and contact
details is added automatically afterwards, and writing the name here
makes it appear twice.

Rules of style:

- Plain, direct, human. Short sentences.
- No marketing language. No exclamation marks.
- Do not use the words: passionate, thrilled, delighted, excited,
  leverage, synergy, dynamic, cutting-edge, world-class, esteemed,
  renowned, prestigious.
- Do not open with "I hope this email finds you well."
- Do not include the student's contact details in the body -- those go
  in a signature which is added separately.
- Address it "Dear Hiring Team," or "Dear {company_name} Team,". Never
  invent a person's name.

Subject line: specific and plain, naming the ask AND the kind of work
this campaign is about, so it does not read as a mass mailing. Examples:

- "Internship enquiry - data analysis, BSc Data Science student"
- "Industrial attachment enquiry - software development"
- "Attachment enquiry - data and reporting support"

Match the subject to the campaign focus given above. No clickbait, no
company flattery, no exclamation marks.
